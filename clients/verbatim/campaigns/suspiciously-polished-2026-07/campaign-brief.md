# Campaign Brief: Suspiciously Polished

**Campaign ID:** `suspiciously-polished-2026-07`
**State backfilled through:** 2026-07-25
**Channel:** Meta
**Client:** Verbatim

## Campaign purpose

Test whether a consumer-facing Verbatim message can convert heavy AI users on Meta.

Core campaign idea:

```text
Does your AI answer look suspiciously polished?
Let's get a second opinion.
```

## Background

The prior consultant campaign failed as a conversion funnel. It produced about 47 outbound clicks, 14 landing-page views, and no AddToChrome events or new users on roughly $50 of spend.

The next hypothesis moves from a professional-risk message to a consumer recognition signal. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, and Perplexity are consumer and prosumer tools. Heavy users may recognize the feeling that an answer is too smooth or too confident before they can explain what is wrong with it.

## Hypothesis

If heavy AI users recognize the feeling of a suspiciously polished answer, they may click to learn how Verbatim has a rival model challenge it.

## Audience

Broad United States audience of heavy daily AI users.

Configured constraints:

- desktop only
- Facebook Feed only
- no consultant-specific targeting
- no enterprise-governance framing

## Emotional payload

```text
Vindication, not risk.
```

Desired user reaction:

```text
I knew that sounded off.
```

## Product wedge

Debate.

Debate is the skepticism layer and starts from the Verbatim V button.

Library is the memory layer. Highlighting saves what the user wants to keep. Do not confuse these behaviors in campaign copy or landing-page content.

## Success interpretation

The first report should determine whether the campaign is operating correctly before it judges the creative.

Operational failures include:

- ad rejection
- broken destination
- no PageView after clicks
- no AddToChromeClick instrumentation
- delivery outside the intended placement
- incorrect creative or copy live

Only after meaningful delivery should the operator recommend `hold`, `investigate`, `pause`, or `iterate`.
